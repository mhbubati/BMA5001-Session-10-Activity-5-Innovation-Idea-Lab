BMA5001 – Innovation Management – Session 10 – Activity 5

💡 The Innovation Idea Lab: Spot a Problem, Design a Better Solution

A guided, practical group activity. Start with a familiar problem, follow the steps, and build one realistic innovation idea.

👋 Your mission

You are a small innovation team. Your job is not to invent something complicated or expensive. Your job is to identify one everyday problem, understand who experiences it, and suggest a simple improvement that creates value.

Remember: An innovation can be a new product, a better service, a simpler process, or a new way of organising work. It does not have to be a world-changing invention.

By the end, your group will have: one clear problem, one target user, one proposed solution, a simple test, and a short pitch.

🧭 A simple guide: what counts as innovation?

🔧ProductA new or improved item
🤝ServiceA better customer experience
⚙️ProcessA faster or easier way to do something
📦Business modelA different way to create or deliver value

Example: A café introducing a reusable cup return scheme is a service and process innovation. It changes how customers borrow, return and reuse cups.

1️⃣ Choose one familiar challenge

Choose ONE challenge below. These examples are deliberately familiar so you can focus on developing the idea rather than trying to understand a complicated industry.

☕

Challenge A: Café queues

At busy times, students wait 10–15 minutes to buy a drink or sandwich. Some leave without buying because they are late for class.

Who is affected? Students and café staff.

Possible direction: Pre-ordering, collection shelf or clearer queue system.

🥗

Challenge B: Food waste

At the end of the day, the café has sandwiches and salads left over, while some students are looking for affordable food.

Who is affected? Students, café managers and the environment.

Possible direction: End-of-day offers, surplus-food alerts or collection bundles.

🗺️

Challenge C: Finding rooms

New students sometimes struggle to find teaching rooms, especially when buildings have similar names or room changes are announced late.

Who is affected? Students, visitors and teaching staff.

Possible direction: A simple campus map, room-finder page or clearer update system.

Need a starting point? Choose the challenge your group understands best. There is no single correct answer. You will be assessed on the logic of your explanation, not on having the most technical idea.

2️⃣ Learn from a worked example

Read this example before answering. Notice how each answer follows logically from the problem.

The problem

Students wait a long time at the café and may miss the start of class.

The user

Students who have only a short break between classes.

The idea

A simple online pre-order form. Students order before their break and collect from a labelled shelf.

Why it helps

Students spend less time queuing. Café staff can prepare orders in batches and organise collection.

How to test it

Try the system for one lunchtime with 20 volunteers and compare waiting times.

What could go wrong?

Orders may be late or incorrect. Start with a limited menu and ask users for feedback.

💡 Copy the thinking pattern, not the exact idea: Problem → User → Solution → Benefit → Small test → Improvement.

3️⃣ Build your group's idea – step by step

Discuss each prompt. Short, clear answers are absolutely fine. Use the sentence starters if you get stuck.

Describe what happens. Avoid jumping straight to the solution.
Be specific: which people, when, and in what situation?
Use what you have observed, experienced or could check. Do not invent statistics.
Explain what it is and how a person would use it.
Think about time, cost, convenience, quality, inclusion or sustainability.
Every idea has limitations. Identify one realistic challenge.
Think of a small trial before spending significant money.

4️⃣ Improve the idea: a quick innovation check

Use these questions to make your idea more realistic. You do not need perfect answers.

5️⃣ Prepare a 60-second group pitch 🎤

Complete the pitch below. One person can present, or each group member can deliver one sentence.

Pitch formula: “We noticed [problem] affecting [users]. We propose [solution], which is a [innovation type]. It will create value by [benefit]. We will test it by [small test].”

🧠 Quick reference: useful innovation vocabulary

TermPlain-English meaningExample
ProblemA difficulty or unmet need.Students wait too long for lunch.
User / customerThe person who experiences the problem or uses the solution.Students with short breaks.
ValueThe improvement or benefit created.Less waiting and less stress.
PrototypeA simple early version used to learn.A paper mock-up of an ordering page.
TestA small trial to gather evidence.Try the process with 20 students.
IterationMaking changes after learning from feedback.Change the collection shelf layout.
⭐ Final reminder: Strong innovation thinking is not about using complicated words. It is about explaining a real problem, proposing a suitable response, and showing how you would learn whether it works.
